In this research we investigate which kinds of concepts are steerable in Llama-3.1-8B and Gemma-2-9B. We use the RFM method to extract steering vectors (or directions) for approximately 600 concepts drawn from 24 WordNet supersense categories, and validate which of them successfully steer the model. We then build a regression model explaining steerability from concept category, concreteness, polysemy, and word frequency, as well as the share of the top eigenvector during extraction ($\lambda_1$/trace). The results help distinguish concepts that behave as single directions --- ``primitive'' for the model --- from those that are composite or context-dependent, and offer practitioners an extraction-time indication of when single-vector steering can be trusted.

\section{Introduction}  % target  (1.0p)

Representation Engineering (RepE) refers to a family of methods that
read and intervene on the internal representations (activations) of 
language models (LMs) to steer their behavior toward or away 
from particular human-interpretable concepts
 \cite{zou2023repe,Wehner2025TaxonomyOpportunitiesChallenges}. 
RepE methods can be split into unsupervised and supervised approaches 
for identifying the relevant directions that represent a concept (which we shall for simplicity call the ``concept vector''). 
In this paper we focus on the latter, and we look specifically at a recent method, Recursive Feature Machines (RFM), which 
outperforms standard concept-vector extraction baselines \cite{beaglehole2026universal}.
Despite being designed as general-purpose, 
all RepE methods including RFM show large variation in which concepts they can
reliably identify and steer. 
Yet to our knowledge, this variation has not been explained in terms of the properties of the concepts themselves.

  \item HERE? The implicit assumption of top-1 eigenvector / PCA-1 / difference-of-means steering: the concept is representable as \emph{one direction}, injectable with \emph{one coefficient}, at whatever layer(s) the method uses. (half para)

\item \textbf{Linear representation hypothesis and steering.} \citet{park2024linear}; RepE \citep{zou2023repe}; CAA \citep{panickssery2024caa}; ActAdd \citep{turner2023actadd}; ITI \citep{li2023iti}; the RFM benchmark we build on \citep{beaglehole2025universal}: kernel ridge + AGOP, top eigenvector as steering vector. \emph{Ours:} we adopt RFM unchanged and ask \emph{which concepts} it works for. Second relation: steering rates are \emph{instrument-relative} --- one changed contrast sentence doubles the rate (19.8\%$\to$41.5\%) at fixed method, model, and judge; method comparisons with hand-tuned per-class prompts inherit this confound. (1 para)
\item \textbf{Multi-dimensional features.} \citet{engels2024notalllinear}: not all features are one-dimensional. \emph{Ours:} we test the premise on a stratified inventory and tie it to word properties: spectrum concentration (PR / $\lambda_1$-share) predicts steering in both models --- ``effectively one-dimensional'' is measurable and graded, and polysemy acts on steerability largely \emph{through} it. (half para)
\item \textbf{Hierarchical concept geometry.} \citet{park2025geometry}: $\sim$900 WordNet concepts, Llama-3 and Gemma; hierarchically related concepts occupy orthogonal subspaces --- the representational-geometry twin of this study. \emph{Ours:} they characterize how hierarchy is \emph{encoded}; we test what it predicts about \emph{intervention}. Our parent-dominance finding is the steering-side counterpart of their parent/child decomposition; and our R4 probe reads their orthogonality causally --- the child-specific residual exists but is rarely independently injectable (9 gained / 14 lost of 150). (half para)
\item \textbf{Predicting steerability.} Directional agreement and geometric predictors of steering (un)reliability \citep{braun2025unreliability,braun2026geometric} --- the closest neighbors to our R3; AxBench \citep{wu2025axbench} for large-scale evaluation. \emph{Ours:} we predict from the concept's \emph{linguistic structure}; the lexical laws (frequency $+$, concreteness $-$, AoA $-$, polysemy $-$) replicate across two models and two instruments --- portable predictors engineering signals cannot supply. We add a caution: geometric predictors are only interpretable relative to the contrast design ($\lambda_1$-share flipped null$\to$significant with the frame). AxBench positioning: they sample concepts \emph{from the model} (SAE-latent labels; no norms, no stratification) and vary methods; we sample from the \emph{lexicon} and fix the method --- only the latter can ask which kinds of concepts steer. Their trained ReFT-r1 bounds our claims: ``unsteerable'' means extraction-based rank-1 steering fails. \textbf{TODO cite:} LAP / logit-lens accessibility (Billa 2026); ``When is your LLM steerable?'' (bib stubs). (1 para)
\item \textbf{Causal identification of concept vectors.} Function vectors \citep{todd2024function}; relation decoding \citep{hernandez2024linearity}; ROME \citep{meng2022rome}. \emph{Ours:} we identify by contrast design, probe by intervention (R4). The depth sweep converges with causal tracing: entity content is injectable in a sharp mid-stack window (depths 14--17 of 32), where tracing localizes subject enrichment --- but read-side statistics cannot substitute for write-side tests. (half para)
\item \textbf{Frequency and linear representations.} Co-occurrence thresholds; long-tail knowledge \citep{kandpal2023longtail}. \emph{Ours:} frequency is a \emph{positive} predictor in both models (univariately in both; surviving covariates only in Llama) --- against our pilot expectation, with co-occurrence-threshold accounts --- and thins when spectrum concentration enters: exposure appears to work partly \emph{through} representational consolidation. (half para)
\item \textbf{LLM--human alignment and psycholinguistic norms.} \citet{shani2025tokens} compare LLM and human concepts on input-side embeddings. \emph{Ours:} mid-stream and causal --- and human structure does predict what can be steered: early-acquired, frequent words steer best (AoA $\beta{=}-0.33/-0.49$ across models), over and above frequency. (half para)
\item \textbf{Methodological caution on cosine.} \citet{you2025semantics}: cosine misleads in anisotropic spaces. \emph{Ours:} geometry claims rest on \emph{differential} cosines under identical measurement, with the between-class value (0.35) as the anisotropy control. (half para)
\item Gap statement: no prior work connects concept-level semantic structure to steering outcome or explains what failure consists of. Our answer: (i) frequency, AoA, polysemy, and concreteness jointly predict steerability, replicating across Llama and Gemma; (ii) failure is usually \emph{informative} --- category/frame delivery, a diffuse spectrum, or injection outside the depth window --- not silence. (half para)

\section{Defining and Sampling Concepts (1.0p)}
\begin{outline}
\item \textbf{Operational definition.} Sidestep the philosophical debate (Murphy 2002; Margolis \& Laurence 1999): a \emph{lexicalized concept} = a meaning a speech community assigned a word to, operationalized as a WordNet synset; each word mapped to its dominant (first) noun sense. (half para)
\item \textbf{Inventory.} All single-word, noun-dominant lemmas from WordNet; category = supersense (26 lexicographer files; Miller 1990; Fellbaum 1998; ``supersense'': Ciaramita \& Johnson 2003). Exclusions: \texttt{noun.Tops}; POS-ambiguous lemmas; small cells sampled exhaustively and flagged. (half para)
\item \textbf{Sample ($\approx$600, flat).} 25/supersense across $\sim$24 usable supersenses; within each supersense, stratify over frequency so category and frequency are not confounded. Six supersenses are pre-specified (before extraction) as the \emph{featured} cells for within-category analyses: \texttt{location}, \texttt{person}, \texttt{feeling} (pilot continuity) and \texttt{artifact}, \texttt{cognition}, \texttt{food} (concreteness extremes). At $n{=}25$ every cell is adequately powered for class means and absorbs attrition (prompted-ceiling/judge exclusions). (1 para)
\item \textbf{Predictors} (one table): Zipf frequency \citep{speer2018wordfreq}; concreteness \citep{brysbaert2014concreteness}; age of acquisition \citep{kuperman2012aoa}; polysemy (\#WordNet senses); supersense; log category size (\#noun synsets in the lexicographer file, covariate). Tested and null: token count (each model's own tokenizer, space-prefixed), verb-sense count. (half para + table)
\item \textbf{Contrast design.} Positives wrap a statement in a universal frame; the frame must be felicitous across \emph{all} supersenses, else frame felicity confounds category; negatives are the bare statements (benchmark-comparable). Rationale for care here: a contrast in which frame, category, and instance always co-occur cannot statistically separate them (see Pilot) --- whatever the frame adds constantly ends up inside every direction. A second negative design --- same frame with a \emph{different} same-supersense concept, which cancels frame and category parent --- is used as the causal probe (R4), not the main contrast. (1 para)
\item \textbf{[NEW jul10] Main-run frame: Frame4 = ``Imagine/assume: \{c\}.''\ (constant prefix, concept unquoted; negatives bare).}\footnote{Frame and Eval numbers preserve our internal run-log identifiers (Frame1 = the benchmark's original class-specific templates); gaps in the numbering are early iterations abandoned before full evaluation.} Selected from a 7-frame grid on the pilot (Table~\ref{tab:framegrid}, Appendix~B): 77/90 Eval1$\cup$Eval6 vs.\ 61 (Frame1 class-specific) and 59 (Frame2b dual frame) --- the only frame at ceiling on moods \emph{and} far ahead on places. Ablations attribute the gain to \emph{assume} (bare-noun reading $\approx$ role-assignment, a class-neutral persona prime) and show quoting the concept costs $\sim$7 points (mention-mode suppresses enactment). Trade-off, quantified in Appendix~B: a constant prefix re-saturates extraction $R^2$ (the prefix is a free class separator), so the dual-frame diagnostics (informative val-$r$) are given up in exchange for steering power; the frame-leak caveat transfers to the imagine/assume register (acknowledged in Limitations).
\item \textbf{Pilot/confirmatory split.} Pilot = 90 concepts (30 each: places, moods, personalities) from the released benchmark lists, used for all exploration; the WordNet sample is confirmatory, untouched during exploration. (half para)
\end{outline}



\section{Method (1.0p)}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig1_opt3.png}
\caption{Study pipeline. Each of the 600 WordNet concepts gets a contrast set
(Frame4 frame positives vs.\ bare-statement negatives), per-layer RFM extraction
(top AGOP eigenvector = steering vector; spectrum retained), steering under two
configurations, and an LLM-judge evaluation under a cued (Eval1) and a neutral (Eval6)
prompt; steerability is then regressed on lexical norms and spectrum shape.}
\label{fig:pipeline}

\begin{outline}
\item \textbf{Direction extraction (RFM) --- and why RFM.} Per layer: kernel ridge regression on concept-vs-negative prompts; AGOP $M = \mathbb{E}[\nabla f \nabla f^{\top}]$; steering vector = top eigenvector of $M$. Chosen among RepE methods because (a) it is the method of the benchmark we build on (comparability); (b) unlike difference-of-means or PCA-1 it returns the \emph{full discriminative spectrum} with eigenvalues --- the spectral metrics below come for free, and the method is self-diagnosing (the pilot's confound was discovered through them); (c) its steering performance matches or beats the simpler extractors on the released benchmark. (1 para)
\item \textbf{Spectral metrics.} $\lambda_1/\mathrm{tr}(M)$, $\lambda_1/\lambda_2$, and the participation ratio $\mathrm{tr}(M)^2/\|M\|_F^2$; per-concept score read at a fixed relative depth (per-layer profile retained; the informative depth is model-specific --- 40\% on Llama, 60\% on Gemma). These measure the \emph{shape} of the discriminative spectrum, not concept identity (see Pilot); they are near-deterministic functions of one another ($|r| \geq .94$), so they enter analyses one at a time. (half para)
\item \textbf{Fixed hyperparameters --- a methodological requirement.} Kernel bandwidth inflates $\lambda_1$/trace mechanically (large bandwidth $\Rightarrow$ near-linear fit $\Rightarrow$ rank-1 AGOP); grid search over near-tied validation correlations injects artifact variance into the dependent measure. All extractions at bw=1, norm=True, reg=$10^{-3}$; vector and metrics read at a fixed (converged) iteration. (half para)
\item \textbf{Two-config steering protocol.} Each concept steered under (i) \emph{all layers}, benchmark defaults (unit vectors, released coefficient grid --- comparability); (ii) \emph{one mid layer} at $\approx$40\% depth (vector scaled by the concept's per-layer projection gap, cf.\ CAA / ITI; effective single-site dose $\approx$1$\times$ local residual-stream norm). $\Delta$(all $-$ single) measures how \emph{distributed} the concept's steerable content is --- with the caveat, added after the depth sweep, that $\Delta$ partly measures \emph{window placement}: the confirmatory single-site arm should sit at depth 15, inside the entity window. Two evaluation prompts (category-inviting Eval1; neutral Eval6; listed verbatim); greedy decoding. (1 para)
\item \textbf{Outcome.} Primary: steered under Eval1 or Eval6 at any coefficient (binary; the union de-biases the evaluation-relativity of either prompt alone, Eval1--Eval6 $\phi{=}0.59$). The binary criterion flattens near-misses (pilot: Edinburgh$\to$``Scottish Highlands'' scores 0); a graded rubric (Eval8) re-judges the same generations as a robustness check (Appendix~E). Verbatim prompts in Appendix~G. (1 para)
\item \textbf{Judge.} gpt-oss:20b (local, on the experiment GPU); symmetric judge question across supersenses. Validated against Claude Sonnet~5 on a 360-item set: 87--91\% agreement, $\kappa = .72$--$.78$; a stricter candidate (Haiku 4.5) was rejected for one-directional divergence from the two-judge consensus. Confirmatory-study additions: re-validation on $\sim$200 WordNet-distribution items + human spot-check of disagreements (Appendix~F). (half para)
\item \textbf{Controls: prompted ceiling.} One unsteered generation per concept (``Tell me about '\{c\}'.'')~+ judge: \textbf{578/600 (Llama) and 586/600 (Gemma) concepts are expressible when simply asked}, and the residual ``failures'' are largely judge strictness on polysemous items (\emph{ray}, \emph{seven}, \emph{junk}); several of them nonetheless \emph{steer}. Ceiling-conditional steering rates are indistinguishable from unconditional (Llama 241/578 = 41.7\% vs.\ 41.5\%). Decisively for the bottom cells: every \texttt{noun.body} concept is expressible (25/25) --- its 2/25 steering rate is a genuine \emph{steering} floor, not an expression floor. Pipeline additionally sanity-checked on known-good concepts per model. (half para)
\item \textbf{Models.} Full protocol on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct; headline analyses replicated on Gemma-2-9B-it. (half para)
\end{outline}

\section{Pilot Diagnostics and Protocol Selection (1.0p)}
% All pilot experiments: N = 30x3 (places/moods/personalities), one 90-concept sample.
% Behavioral arms re-run at N=90 pending; interim n=15 shakedown numbers are provisional.
\begin{outline}
\item \textbf{Hyper-parameter selection (summary).} Validation $r$ saturates ($\sim$0.97--0.99) across bandwidths, statement counts, and iterations $\Rightarrow$ selection by validation is third-decimal noise; large bandwidth forces rank-1 AGOP by construction; converged readout cuts metric instability 3--4$\times$. Decision rule: fixed modal setting, converged readout. Details in Appendix~A. (NB the saturation itself is frame-structural: any constant-prefix contrast hands the classifier a free separator; dual frames de-saturate val-$r$ --- Appendix~B.) (half para)
\item \textbf{Finding 1: parent-dominance.} Different same-category concepts' extracted top-1 directions are nearly identical (places: cosine $0.97 \to 0.84 \to 0.72$ across depth; \emph{all} top-3 components $\geq$0.90 shared). Single-layer dose-response saturates at the category \emph{prototype} (every city $\to$ ``quiet beach at sunset'') with region-level near-misses (Edinburgh$\to$Dublin pub); giving secondary components injection mass adds a generic roleplay axis, not instance content. Three legs: geometry, dose-response, component ablation. Interpretation: a contrast in which frame, parent, and instance always co-occur is statistically one axis --- what steering delivers is mostly the category. (1 para + figure \texttt{parent\_share\_by\_depth.png})
\item \textbf{Finding 2: coherence $\neq$ identity.} Places show the \emph{highest} band $\lambda_1$/trace yet fail single-layer steering; moods (lower) succeed. Spectral metrics certify that a \emph{coherent} (context-invariant, rank-1) direction was extracted --- not that it is the judged content. Consequence: their regression coefficients are expected to be conditional on contrast type; tested under both contrasts. (1 para)
\item \textbf{Finding 3: attribute vs.\ instance.} Attribute concepts (moods) steer from one mid layer; instance identities steer only under all-layer injection, reaching region granularity --- consistent with multi-stage assembly (per-layer residue accumulation + late lexical tipping + autoregressive bootstrap once the name is emitted). Motivates the two-config protocol and $\Delta$. (1 para)
\item \textbf{Protocol justifications} (one line each; evidence in Appendix~C): single mid layer in the mid-stack behavioral window --- the depth sweep (Discussion) later located it precisely at depths 14--17 for entity concepts, with the original 40\% (depth-13) site just below its onset; 60\% yields token-bias then repetition collapse. No top-$k$ arms --- the top-3 subspace under the released contrast is category-shared. Coefficient $\approx$1$\times$ local residual norm via per-layer magn scaling. Granularity as secondary outcome --- the binary judge flattens near-misses. (1 para)
\end{outline}

\item \textbf{R1 instrument comparison (Frame2b arm): the frame moves the rate, much less the ordering.} Under the dual Frame2b frame the same protocol yields 119/600 (19.8\%; spread 2--9); switching to Frame4 improves 22 of 24 supersenses while the ranking stays broadly stable (feeling top, body bottom under both; \texttt{noun.body} immovable at 2/25; Figure~\ref{fig:supersense}, Appendix~B). \item \textbf{R2: Psycholinguistic predictors (Table~\ref{tab:reg600v4}): same signs in both models; all four lexical predictors significant in Llama, three of four in Gemma.} Llama: conc $-0.20^{*}$, zipf $+0.28^{*}$, AoA $-0.33^{**}$, polysemy $-0.30^{**}$; Gemma: conc $-0.20^{*}$, zipf $+0.17$ (positive, n.s.), AoA $-0.49^{**}$, polysemy $-0.35^{**}$. \textbf{The pilot expectation (concreteness $+$, polysemy carrying the signal, frequency null) is rejected: frequent-but-abstract concepts steer best.} AoA and polysemy were null under Frame2b and surface once the instrument improves (Frame2b: zipf $+0.40^{*}$, conc $-0.28^{*}$; Appendix~B). Honest-reporting nuance (Figure~\ref{fig:covariates}): polysemy and concreteness do \emph{not} separate univariately (MW $p{>}.6$; effects conditional on correlated covariates); zipf, AoA, PR separate cleanly in both models ($p{<}.005$). Collinearity within the fitted specification is benign in both models (all VIF $\le 2.7$; the largest is zipf's, driven by zipf--token-count $r{\approx}-.70$ --- Zipf's law of abbreviation; conc--zipf $r{=}.09$); the only strong collinearity is among the spectral metrics themselves ($r{\le}-.93$), which is why they enter one-at-a-time. \textbf{[NEW jul11] Token count is null throughout} (each model's own tokenizer, space-prefixed): it does not separate steerable from non-steerable univariately (MW $p{=}.16$ Llama, $.074$ Gemma) and its coefficient is n.s.\ in every specification (Table~\ref{tab:reg600v4}) --- the frequency effect is not tokenization length. \textbf{Honest note: Gemma's zipf was $+0.23^{\dagger}$ when the token-count covariate mistakenly used Llama's tokenizer; correcting it to Gemma's own tokenizer moves zipf to $+0.17$ n.s.\ --- the coefficient is fragile and we do not claim it.} Log category size is a positive predictor in Llama ($+0.31^{**}$ with the lexical block; $+0.29^{**}$ surviving $\lambda_1$/trace) but null in Gemma ($-0.01$) --- reported as a non-replicating candidate, not among the laws. (1 para + table)
\item \textbf{R3: Spectral metrics: every spectral metric carries signal, and each alone beats the whole lexical block.} Llama, one-at-a-time: $\lambda_1$/trace $+0.55^{**}$ (AIC 778.5), $\lambda_1/\lambda_2$ $+0.57^{**}$ (775.3), PR $-0.49^{**}$ (786.5) vs.\ lexical block 799.0, null 816.4; best model lex$+\lambda_1$/trace (750.0, $+0.69^{**}$ at the depth-15 anchor; the PR variant: 745.9, $-0.74^{**}$). \textbf{[NEW jul11] Absolute predictability:} the full model separates steerable from non-steerable concepts at cross-validated AUC $0.70$ (Llama, depth-15 anchor) / $0.66$ (Gemma) --- clearly above the coin-flip $0.5$, and $\lambda_1$/trace alone reaches $0.66$ on Llama (5-fold CV) --- but modest in absolute terms: the claim is that steerability variance is \emph{systematic}, not that it is fully predictable from word-level properties. Concentrated discriminative spectra steer; polysemy is largely absorbed by PR in the joint model --- polysemous words appear to fail \emph{via} diffuse spectra. \textbf{Instrument caution (one of our sharpest methodological findings): under the dual Frame2b frame $\lambda_1$/trace was \emph{null} --- the register axis parked in within-positive variance can capture the top eigenvector for weak concepts (Appendix~B), corrupting spectral readouts. Spectral diagnostics are only interpretable relative to the contrast design.} Spectral metrics are near-collinear ($r{\le}-.94$): one-at-a-time only. \textbf{The read depth for the spectral signal is model-dependent, and is anchored to the model's steering window rather than chosen in-sample:} regressing lex$+\lambda_1$/trace at every depth, the signal plateaus at 40--53\% in Llama but is null until $\sim$47\% in Gemma with a window at 57--70\% --- a common 50\% convention would keep Llama's signal but erase Gemma's ($+0.17^{\dagger}$). \textbf{[NEW jul12] Llama's read depth is now anchored at depth 15 (46\%) --- the peak of its single-layer steering window in the $n{=}600$ confirmatory sweep (268/600 at depth 15) --- an independent, write-side criterion (``read the spectrum where the model steers'').} \textbf{[Bold note: Gemma re-anchor pending its 600-concept sweep (running).]} (1 para)
\item \textbf{[NEW jul12] R3b: Window-corrected steerability --- 327/600 (54.5\%) --- and the spectral predictor strengthens (Table~\ref{tab:reg600union}).} The $n{=}600$ confirmatory depth sweep shows single-layer depth 15 alone steers 268/600 (44.7\%), \emph{exceeding} the all-layer benchmark (249), with complementary coverage: 78 concepts steer at depth 15 but not all-layer (entity-window classes: location, attribute, substance), 59 the reverse (multi-site persona classes: person, animal). Treating ``steers under either protocol'' as the outcome, $\lambda_1$/trace rises to $+0.83^{**}$ while the lexical coefficients thin --- correcting for injection site makes extraction quality the dominant remaining factor. (Upper bound on window-miss: the union over all ten depths reaches 369/600, but a 10-way union accumulates judge noise and is not multiple-comparison corrected --- not a headline number.) (half para + table)
\item \textbf{Category is partly mediated by measurable word properties.} Supersense dummies beat the null (AIC 784.1 vs.\ 816.4) but lose to lexical$+\lambda_1$/trace (750.0), and adding them to the best model does not pay: the category signal visible in R1 is substantially carried by the continuous predictors. (Under Frame2b the dummies did not even beat the null; the wider Frame4 spread carries real categorical signal.) (half para)
\item \textbf{R4: The rescue probe (causal).} Design: for failing concepts, cancel the frame/parent component --- either by re-extracting with within-category contrasts (``loves Edinburgh'' vs.\ ``loves \{other city\}''; not yet run) or by subtracting the leave-one-out supersense mean from the saved direction ($v_{\mathrm{spec}}$; executed, $n{=}150$). \textbf{Outcome: no net rescue (25 vs.\ 30 of 150 steer; McNemar $p{\approx}.4$) --- shared-component dominance is not the prevailing failure mode, though the subtraction doubles as a frame-leak control.} (Full $v_{\mathrm{spec}}$ material retired from the appendix jul12; record in RUNLOG 013 and the pre-deletion appendix backup.) (1 para)
\item \textbf{[NEW jul12] R4b: Adding a second direction does not rescue either ($K{=}2$ probe, $n{=}600$).} Eigenvalue-weighted top-2 eigenvectors at the window peak (depth 15; combination unit-normalized so the injected magnitude and coefficients match the $K{=}1$ arm exactly): 275/600 vs.\ 268/600 --- 45 gained, 38 lost, McNemar exact $p{=}.51$. The flips are the borderline stratum (lower frequency and spectral concentration than stable concepts) and their direction is chance; a one-layer shift produces the same churn (Appendix~D, Fig.~\ref{fig:k2flips}). Read jointly with R4: neither \emph{subtracting} the shared component ($v_{\mathrm{spec}}$) nor \emph{adding} a second component rescues failing concepts --- what unsteerable concepts lack is injectable content, not rank. (half para)
\item \textbf{[NEW jul13] R4c: The common component is load-bearing drive, not mere contamination (PCA subtraction, executed).} PC1 of the 600 extracted directions (60\% of the second-moment spectrum; no WordNet grouping --- the ``All-but-the-Top'' operation of \citealp{mu2018allbutthetop}) removed from each depth-15 vector: steering drops to 234/600 vs.\ 268 baseline (42 gained / 76 lost; McNemar exact $p{=}.002$ --- the one \emph{significant} flip asymmetry in the rescue series, and it is negative). The damage is class-structured: abstract/affect classes lose (event $-6$, attribute/person/group $-5$, feeling $-4$: \emph{wrath}, \emph{sulk}, \emph{hush}, \emph{sincerity}\dots) while concrete/specific classes gain (food $+4$: \emph{marmalade}, \emph{ricotta}, \emph{carp}, \emph{dandelion}\dots). Reading: the shared component supplies generic topical \emph{drive} that abstract concepts ride, while \emph{masking} the specific content of concrete ones --- the causal counterpart, with matching sign, of the mediation finding that global-axis loading suppresses concrete concepts. \texttt{nb\_pca\_subtraction.ipynb}. (half para)
\item \textbf{R5: Distributedness.} $\Delta$(all-layer $-$ single-layer): single-layer depth-13 (magn dose) 90/600 (15.0\%) vs.\ all-layer 249 (41.5\%), ratio 2.8$\times$ (Frame2b: 3.5$\times$); 11 concepts steer \emph{only} single-layer (over-steer rescues; abstract/attribute skew). Caveat from the depth sweep: depth 13 sits below the entity window, so $\Delta$ partly measures window placement --- interpret jointly with the sweep (Discussion). (half para)
\item \textbf{R6: Generality (Gemma-2-9B replication): rates and laws replicate; item convergence is itself instrument-dependent.} Rates: 224/600 (37.3\%) vs.\ Llama 249 (41.5\%); the Frame4 gain replicates ($\sim$2$\times$ both models' Frame2b rates). Laws: all four lexical signs replicate (AoA $-0.49^{**}$ strongest); PR replicates \emph{at Gemma's informative depth} (60--70\%: $-0.42^{**}$; null at Llama's 40\% convention) --- \emph{same law, model-specific depth}. Items: per-concept $\phi = 0.43$ (154 shared) and supersense-profile $r = 0.44$, both up from Frame2b (0.33 / 0.12 n.s.): part of the earlier item-divergence was instrument noise, though which concepts steer remains substantially model-relative (Gemma tops: event/phenomenon/group; body bottom in both). Supporting pilot evidence: the $\lambda_1$/trace class ordering \emph{inverts} across models (Llama places $>$ moods; Gemma the reverse) --- spectral values are model-geometry-laden; compare within-model only. Gemma dose grid [14,17,20,23] set by dose-response audit (Appendix~C). (1 para)
\end{outline}

\section{Discussion (1.0p)}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{dag_steerability.png}
\caption{The causal structure we read our results through. Dashed borders are
latent (unobserved) variables, blue are observed, amber are
experimenter-controlled. Psycholinguistic norms have no causal path into the
model --- they predict steering only as proxies of the shared latent; the
spectrum statistics are instrument-dependent summaries of the extracted
direction, not the encoding itself; the dashed path marks the judge confound
(the judge's own pretraining --- a different corpus, but correlated exposure
through the same underlying language).}
\label{fig:dag}
\end{figure}

\begin{outline}
\item What makes a concept steerable: attributes (context-general colorings) admit a single injectable direction; instance identities require assembled, multi-stage content --- steering failure is usually \emph{delivery of the parent category}, not silence. (1 para)
\item \textbf{What the spectral metrics are: an intervention-match score, with an instrument caveat.} Spectrum \emph{concentration} (low PR / high $\lambda_1$-share) measures how well the concept's discriminative signature fits the one move single-vector steering can make (one constant vector, all contexts); it predicts steering in both models. \textbf{The mechanism behind R2's reversal: \emph{abstract} (not concrete) concepts steer better} --- for concrete instance-like concepts the dominant direction is largely the shared category axis, so its coherence does not deliver the instance (parent-dominance). \textbf{And the caveat: spectral diagnostics of extracted directions are only interpretable relative to the contrast design that produced them} --- under the dual Frame2b frame $\lambda_1$/trace read as null because a frame-register axis could capture the top eigenvalue slot (Appendix~B); under the constant Frame4 prefix it predicts steering with the expected sign. (1 para)
\item \textbf{[NEW jul12] A causal reading of the results (Figure~\ref{fig:dag}).} The concept's role in human language (latent) drives both the psycholinguistic norms --- observed proxies with \emph{no causal path into the model} --- and the concept's distribution in pretraining (latent), which determines whether the model allocates it a compact subspace (latent). The extracted direction $v_c$ is a \emph{joint} product of that allocation and the extraction contrast (the frame-leak arrow is the Frame2b lesson); the spectrum statistics are summary statistics of $v_c$; the outcome depends on $v_c$ and the injection design. Two consequences: (i) lexical coefficients surviving spectral controls is expected even under full mediation, because $\lambda_1$/trace is a noisy, instrument-dependent indicator of the latent allocation; (ii) the judge is itself an LLM whose \emph{own} pretraining (a different corpus, but the same underlying language) descends from the same latent, so rare concepts are rare for the judge too (dashed path); this is bounded empirically by the prompted-ceiling control and by cross-judge agreement --- gpt-oss and Claude Sonnet produce identical per-class breakdowns on a fixed 360-item set (Appendix~F), and our aggregates agree with the RFM paper's ChatGPT-judged results. K$>$1's failure is a statement about the outcome node's structural equation: injection must match the allocation's weighting, possibly non-linearly, not merely its rank. (1 para + figure)
\item \textbf{[NEW jul12] Mediation probe (executed): in Llama, concreteness works through the direction's \emph{global}-axis loading --- not the WordNet-category axis.} Each direction's shared-component loading decomposes into a global axis (sign-aligned mean of all 600 directions; mean $|\cos| = 0.82$ --- a frame/register component) and a category-specific residual share (mean $0.45$). Adding both to the lexical regression absorbs concreteness ($-0.20^{*} \to -0.08$ n.s.) via the global share ($-0.42^{**}$; AIC $799 \to 783$), while the category-specific share carries nothing ($-0.01$). This is direct statistical evidence for the dominance mechanism, but it relocates it: what eats concrete concepts at the read depth is loading on a corpus-generic axis, not the supersense parent. Two caveats on the category null: the WordNet grouping \emph{does} pick out real structure in the model's geometry (within-supersense residual alignment $0.45$ vs.\ ${\approx}0.01$ for random directions), so the construct is model-meaningful --- but its share not predicting steering may still reflect supersense-level parents being too \emph{coarse} (the qualitative failures deliver fine-grained parents: \emph{city} for Edinburgh, not \emph{location}); a finer-grained hypernym grouping is the natural follow-up. \textbf{Does not replicate in Gemma} (global share $+0.17^{\dagger}$, opposite sign; concreteness unchanged) --- reported as a Llama finding. \texttt{nb\_axis\_mediation.py}. (1 para)
\item \textbf{Measured steerability is instrument-dependent --- and so is cross-model generality.} Changing one frame sentence doubles the steerable share in both models (19.8$\to$41.5\% Llama; 18.8$\to$37.3\% Gemma) and \emph{raises} cross-model item agreement ($\phi$ 0.33$\to$0.43) and category-profile agreement ($r$ 0.12$\to$0.44). Absolute rates from any single protocol should not be read as properties of the model alone; but the \emph{laws} (frequency $+$, concreteness $-$, AoA $-$, polysemy $-$, spectrum concentration $-$) survive instrument change and model change in sign (frequency's joint-model coefficient clears significance only in Llama), which is the paper's strongest generality claim. (half para)
\item \textbf{Depth structure: concepts live in depth windows, ordered by abstractness.} Attributes are injectable from $\sim$30\% depth onward with a broad plateau; entity identities switch on sharply at $\sim$45\% (window 14--17 of 32) and fade late; personas need multi-site injection. One well-chosen layer nearly matches all-layer steering (71 vs.\ 77 of 90) --- ``all layers'' mostly buys window coverage. Read-side extraction statistics do not locate the window (they miss the late decline entirely): injectability is a write property. (half para)
\item Status of extracted vectors: the residual stream has no privileged basis, so extracted directions are \emph{experiment-relative}; within-model relational claims are valid, coordinate-level and raw cross-model comparisons are not. Anchored spaces (embedding/unembedding, MLP neurons) are where mechanism pins a frame. \textbf{[NEW jul12] Future work: re-pose the extraction in an anchored basis --- MLP neurons --- where both the \emph{identity} of the injected object (which computational units carry the concept) and its \emph{dose} (units with natural activation scales) become basis-meaningful, rather than experiment-relative rotations of the residual stream.} (half para)
\item Two routes to a vector's identity: \emph{experiment design} (contrasts that vary exactly one thing) vs.\ \emph{causal mediation} (function vectors, ROME). Pipeline: propose by contrast, accept by causal test --- R4 instantiates it. (half para)
\item Practical implications for RePE: report spectral concentration and contrast design alongside steering benchmarks; stratified concept inventories; granularity-graded judging over binary. (half para)
\end{outline}

\subsection*{The depth axis: first-steerable-depth sweep (0.5p)}
% Framing: besides HOW MANY directions compose a concept (the spectral axis),
% WHERE in depth it becomes injectable is a second, near-orthogonal axis.
% Executed on the pilot; WordNet extension is future work.



\begin{outline}
\item \textbf{Design.} Pilot 90 (moods/places/personalities) $\times$ every single-layer depth 2--31 (all layers except the very first and last), magn dose, Frame4 directions, Eval1$\cup$Eval6 (Figure~\ref{fig:depthsweep}). \textbf{[NEW jul12: sweep extended from 10--19 to the full 2--31 grid.]} (half para)
\item \textbf{Result 1: entity concepts have a sharp onset at depth 14 (of 32).} Places: 0--2/30 at every depth $\le$13, then \textbf{22/30 at depth 14}, window 14--17, then a low tail (3--9) through the twenties and 0 by depth 31. This dissolves the earlier ``places don't steer single-layer'' conclusion: the old two-site protocol (13/19) \emph{straddled the window}. (half para)
\item \textbf{Result 2: nothing steers in the first quarter of the network, and the three classes have nested windows.} Depths 2--7 are dead for all classes (a 3--5 concept trickle of moods, floor noise). Onsets are ordered by abstractness --- personalities and moods switch on at depth 8--9 (1$\to$14 and 4$\to$9), places at 14 --- and so are the closing edges, in reverse: personalities collapse first (0 from depth 23), places fade through the twenties, moods decay last and most gracefully (plateau 25--30 from depth 13 to $\sim$20, still 10+ at depth 27, 4 at depth 31). Personalities never find a strong single-layer home (peak 20 at depths 11/14) --- persona content appears genuinely multi-site. (half para)
\item \textbf{Result 3: one well-chosen layer $\approx$ all layers} (71/90 at depth 15 vs.\ 77/90 all-layer): the all-layer advantage is largely \emph{not knowing the window}, not distributed injection per se (except personalities). Per-layer read-side metrics rise into the window but entirely miss the late decline ($|r| \approx .5$--$.6$): first-steerable-depth is a \emph{write} property. (half para)
\item \textbf{Future work:} same sweep on the six featured WordNet cells (150 concepts) with taxonomic depth as ground truth (hypernyms shallower than hyponyms?); independence from the spectral axis assessed within supersense; compositional vignette --- is direction(``strict father'') $\approx \alpha\,$direction(``strict'') $+ \beta\,$direction(``father'')? (half para)
\end{outline}

\section{Limitations (0.5p; uncounted in ACL format)}
\begin{outline}
\item Single extraction method (RFM); $\lambda_1$/trace is AGOP-specific --- PCA explained-variance as cross-check (appendix if time). (half para)
\item Steering conclusions are relative to the additive intervention class; clamp/projection interventions may behave differently (targets saved, untested). (half para)
\item LLM judge (validated but imperfect); English only; dominant-sense assumption; norms are human ratings. (half para)
\item Correlational core with one causal probe (R4); extraction-design effects and model limits not fully separable outside that design. (half para)
\item Depth-sweep evidence is pilot-only ($n{=}90$, 3 concept classes, one model); the WordNet-wide sweep and the Gemma sweep are future work. Measured rates are instrument-relative (quantified, but a reader should not quote ``41.5\%'' as a model constant). (half para)
\item The Frame4 frame's constant prefix puts an imagine/assume register component into every direction; the frame-leak control ($v_{\mathrm{spec}}$ subtraction) was run on the Frame2b instrument only and not repeated for Frame4 --- some Frame4 successes may be partly frame-carried. (half para)
\end{outline}
